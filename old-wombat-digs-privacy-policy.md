# Old Wombat Digs: Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 28, 2026

Old Wombat Digs is a puzzle game for Android phones and tablets and for iPhone and iPad, made by Matanya Asis, trading as As Is ("we", "us"). We are responsible for the information described here. Contact: matanya.asis288@gmail.com.

The short version: the game is free and shows ads through Google AdMob (full-screen ads between some levels and, after the first ten levels, a small banner under the game); those ads come from Google or, through AdMob, from AppLovin or Meta Audience Network, which collect some device data to show and measure them. A one-time "Remove ads" purchase turns the ads off. Meta's and Tenjin's SDKs measure our own ads for the game (on Facebook, Instagram, Google and inside other apps): the install and a few moments such as level 10 cleared (see "Measuring our own ads" below). Your progress is saved on your device and, if you use Google Play Games (Android) or iCloud (iPhone and iPad), backed up to your own account there, so it follows you to a new phone. The game adds to anonymous daily totals on our game server (like "level 12 finished today") that can't be traced to anyone. In an online duel the other player sees your moves and, if you choose one, your duel name. The game has no accounts, and we don't keep any personal information about you.

## Accepting this policy

When you first open the game, it shows a short card that links to this policy and asks you to agree before you play. The card comes back once each time this policy changes. Ads, the measuring of our own ads and the anonymous totals described below start only after you have agreed (the cloud save in your own Google Play Games or iCloud account works from the start, as part of those accounts), and Google's consent form (where the law requires one) and Apple's tracking question come after the card. Your answer is saved on your device only. You can read this policy again at any time from "Privacy policy" in the game's Settings.

## What the game saves

- **Your game progress.** The levels you've finished, your best number of swipes and stars for each level, your settings (such as sound and vibration), your duel name if you chose one, which version of this policy you agreed to, and whether you bought "Remove ads" are saved on your device, so the game remembers where you were. Apart from the cloud copy in your own account described below, this never leaves your device through the game, and it says nothing about who you are.
- **Device backup.** If device backup is on (Android backup with your Google account, or iCloud backup on iPhone and iPad), the backup may include this small save file. That backup is handled by Google or Apple under your own account, not by us, and we can't see it.
- **Deleting it.** "Reset progress" in the game's Settings clears your progress. Uninstalling the game deletes everything it saved on the device.

## Cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard

These are optional. The game plays the same without them.

- **Android (Google Play Games).** If you have a Google Play Games profile, the game can sign you in to it (automatically, or when you tap "Sign in to Play Games" in the game's Settings). It then keeps a small copy of your progress (the levels you've finished, with your best number of swipes and stars for each, the opals you've found and earned, and the wombat looks and duel powers you've bought with them) in Play Games' Saved Games under your own Google Account, so you can carry on from another phone or tablet. Your duel name and settings are not in it. It also tells Play Games which of the game's achievements you earned and your star count for the game's leaderboard, where other players can see it next to your Play Games gamer name. The game reads your gamer name only to show it to you in Settings. Google keeps all this as described in the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). We don't receive it and can't see it.
- **iPhone and iPad (iCloud and Game Center).** If your device is signed in to iCloud, the game keeps the same small copy of your progress (levels, opals, looks and powers) in iCloud key-value storage under your Apple Account. The game never asks you to sign in for this. If your device is signed in to Game Center, the game tells Game Center which achievements you earned and your star count for the leaderboard, where other players can see it next to your Game Center nickname, as your Game Center settings allow. Apple keeps all this as described in Apple's Privacy Policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). We don't receive it and can't see it.
- **Deleting it.** On Android, delete Old Wombat Digs' data from your Play Games profile (in the Play Games app, or in your phone's Settings > Google > Play Games), or delete your whole Play Games profile in your Google Account. On iPhone and iPad, delete the game's data in Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Account Storage, and remove the game from Game Center in Settings > Game Center. "Reset progress" in the game's Settings also clears the levels and opals found in the cloud copy (the looks and powers you bought stay yours); scores and achievements already sent stay with Play Games or Game Center until you delete them there.

## Ads

The game shows ads through Google AdMob: full-screen ads between some levels (none on the first ten levels, and after that one every few levels you finish or fail), and, once you have finished the tenth level, a small banner ad in a strip of its own under the level list and under the burrow. No ad ever covers the burrow while you are digging.

Each ad comes from Google or from one of two other ad networks that AdMob asks for an ad at the same time ("mediation"), whichever offers the best ad for that moment:

- **Google** (Google LLC), through AdMob,
- **AppLovin** (AppLovin Corporation),
- **Meta Audience Network** (Meta Platforms, Inc.), Meta's network for ads inside apps.

To show ads, limit how often you see them, measure them and prevent fraud, these ad networks may collect from your device:

- your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers,
- your IP address and the approximate location derived from it,
- information about the ads you see and how you interact with them,
- diagnostic and performance information about the ad service.

Each network handles this information as its own policy describes: Google in "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services": https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and in the Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy; AppLovin in its Privacy Policy: https://www.applovin.com/privacy; Meta in its Privacy Policy: https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy. The other two networks are only asked for an ad, and are only started, after the consent step described below, and they get the same consent answer as Google. We don't receive this information and can't link it to you.

**Your choices**

- Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent before any ad is requested, and the consent form lists every ad network and partner. You can change your choice at any time from "Privacy settings" in the game's Settings, and the change applies to all three networks.
- On iPhone and iPad, the game asks for permission to track (Apple's App Tracking Transparency) before any personalized ads; if you choose "Ask App Not to Track", you only see non-personalized ads (the game tells Google, AppLovin and Meta Audience Network your answer), and the measuring of our own ads (Meta's and Tenjin's, see below) gets no advertising ID. You can change this anytime in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
- On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your device's settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads, depending on your device). Once deleted, the measuring of our own ads (Meta's and Tenjin's, see below) gets no advertising ID either.
- The one-time "Remove ads" purchase turns the ads off.

## Measuring our own ads

We advertise Old Wombat Digs on Facebook and Instagram (Meta), on Google (Google Ads), and inside other apps and games (AppLovin). To know whether those ads work, and so the ad networks can show our ads to people who are likely to enjoy the game, the game tells two measurement services about the install and a few moments in the game. Nothing here changes which ads you see in Old Wombat Digs, there is no sign-in, and the "Remove ads" purchase doesn't change this, as it shows no ads.

**What is sent, besides the install and each time the game is opened:** only these moments, each once per install, by name: the first lessons done (level 3 cleared), levels 10, 25 and 50 cleared, the game opened again the day after it was installed, the first duel begun, and the "Remove ads" purchase with its list price. Nothing else about how you play is sent: no levels in progress, swipes, scores, opals, duel moves, names or room codes.

**Who:** both services are turned on only after the consent step at first launch, and on iPhone and iPad only after you have answered Apple's tracking question. Where the law requires consent first (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), they aren't turned on at all.

**Meta.** The game includes Meta's SDK (Meta Platforms, Inc.).

- **What Meta receives:** that the game was opened and the moments above, the time, the game's version, basic device information (model and system version), your IP address, and your device's advertising ID: on iPhone and iPad only if you allowed tracking (if you chose "Ask App Not to Track", it is not sent, and Meta only measures through Apple's privacy-preserving SKAdNetwork and Meta's Aggregated Event Measurement); on Android unless you deleted the advertising ID in your device's settings.
- **What it's used for:** measuring how many people installed and played the game after seeing one of our ads, and letting Meta show our ads to people like those who enjoyed it. Meta handles this information as described in Meta's Privacy Policy (https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy) and may use it as described there. We see only totals in Meta's ads tools, never information about you.
- **Your choices:** the tracking permission on iPhone and iPad (Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking), and on Android deleting your advertising ID in your device's settings. You can also manage how Meta uses information from other apps in your Facebook or Instagram settings ("Your activity off Meta technologies").

**Tenjin, for our ads on Google and inside other apps and games.** Ads shown through Google Ads and AppLovin (AppLovin Corporation) are measured by a service in the advertised app. For this the game includes Tenjin's SDK (Tenjin, Inc.), and on iPhone and iPad also Google's on-device conversion measurement, which Tenjin reads.

- **What Tenjin receives:** that the game was opened and the moments above, the time, the game's version, basic device information (model, system version, language, country and time zone settings), your IP address, on Android how the game was installed (Google Play's install referrer), and on iPhone and iPad the identifier Apple gives our apps on your device (the IDFV, which only our apps can use). Your device's advertising ID: on iPhone and iPad only if you allowed tracking; on Android unless you deleted it in your device's settings.
- **Who else:** when an install came from one of our ads, Tenjin tells the ad network that showed it (Google or AppLovin) that the install and those moments happened, with the advertising ID only where it was sent to Tenjin, so the network can count and improve that campaign. On iPhone and iPad, if you chose "Ask App Not to Track", the networks measure through Apple's privacy-preserving SKAdNetwork and AdAttributionKit instead: the game sets Apple's small "conversion value" from the moments above, and Apple sends it to the ad network (and a copy to Tenjin) without any identifier or anything about you. Google's on-device measurement works on the device and sends Google no identifier.
- **What it's used for:** measuring how many people installed and played the game after seeing one of our ads, so we can tell which ads are worth running and the networks can show them to people likely to enjoy the game. Tenjin handles this information for us as our service provider, as described in its Privacy Policy (https://tenjin.com/privacy-policy/), Google as described in the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy), and AppLovin as described in its Privacy Policy (https://www.applovin.com/privacy). We see only totals in their tools, never information about you.
- **Your choices:** the same tracking permission on iPhone and iPad, and on Android deleting your advertising ID. To ask about or delete what Tenjin holds about your device, email us and we'll pass the request to Tenjin.

## Playing a friend online (Dig Duel)

In "Multiplayer" you can play a Dig Duel against a friend on another phone: one of you creates a room and gets a four-letter code, the other types it in. This is optional; the rest of the game, and a duel on one shared phone, work without it. Online duels use Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), a hosting service we use only to pass the duel's messages between the two phones in a room. Our Supabase project is in Frankfurt, Germany.

- **What is sent while you are in a room:** the room's four-letter code, a random ID made fresh each time the app starts (not linked to you or your device), your duel name if you chose one, which version of the duel your app speaks, when you came into the room, and the duel itself: which burrow is played, who swipes first, the direction of each swipe with a check number so both phones agree, and whether you ask for a rematch or leave. Your device's IP address is seen by the server to make the connection, as with any internet service.
- **Your duel name (optional).** You can choose a name for online duels, up to 12 letters. It is sent to the other player in your room while you are in it, and it is not stored on our server; it is saved only on your own device (not in the cloud save, the anonymous totals or any log). Please don't use your real full name. A word filter refuses rude names when you type them, and a rude name arriving from another phone is shown as "Friend". If you don't choose a name, the other player sees "Friend".
- **Report and hide.** Tap the other player's name to hide it on your device (it shows as "Friend" from then on) and, if you like, to report it: your device's email app opens a message to us with the name and the room's code, which you can send or not. If you send it, we receive your email address and what the message says, use them only to look at the report (for example to add a word to the filter), and delete the email once it is dealt with.
- **No chat.** There is no sign-in and no chat in a duel; the only things you type are the room's code and your duel name. The other player sees your moves and your duel name.
- **Who sees it:** the other player in the same room. Anyone who knows a room's code can join it while it is open, so give a code only to the person you want to play.
- **How long it is kept:** the messages are passed on live and are gone when the room closes. We don't store them in a database, and we don't use them for ads or profiles. Supabase may keep short-lived technical logs (such as connection records) for security, as described at https://supabase.com/privacy.

## Reminders

If you allow notifications, the game may show up to two friendly reminders when you haven't played for a while: one 2 days after you last played and one 5 days after, then nothing more until you play again (for example "Level 43 is waiting for you"). They are set by the game on your own device from your saved progress: no server sends them, and nothing about you leaves your device for them. Opening the game takes them away. Your device asks before the game can show any (after you finish level 5, never on first launch), and you can turn them off at any time with "Reminders" in the game's Settings, or in your device's settings.

## Anonymous totals

To see how the game is played and where it needs work, the game adds one to a daily total on our Supabase server (the same hosting service as for online duels, in Frankfurt, Germany) when something happens: the game opened, a phone's first play of the day (with whether it is the phone's first day with the game, the day after, or about how many days later), whether music, sounds or vibration are off, whether "Remove ads" is owned and whether the banner is showing, a level or chess level started, finished (and whether for the first time, with three stars or with opal stars), failed or left, the Chess or Multiplayer screen opened, a room made or joined, a duel started, finished or left (on one phone or online), a full-screen ad shown or one that wasn't ready, the Daily Dig started, each of its cards solved, missed or run out of time, its score at the end and when it is shared, and "Remove ads" tapped, bought or restored.

The Daily Dig's Share button opens your device's own share sheet with a short text (the date, ten coloured squares, your score and a link to the game in the store). You choose where it goes, and the game sends nothing more with it.

All that is kept is the day, what happened, the level number where there is one, whether it was Android or iPhone/iPad, the game's version, and the running total. No ID, name, room code or device details go with it, so a total looks the same whoever added to it and can't be linked to you or to anyone else. The days your phone needs for this (its first day with the game and the last day it counted) stay on your device and are never sent. Your device's IP address is seen by the server to make the connection, as with any internet service; we don't store it with the totals or use it. We use the totals only to improve the game.

## Purchases

The optional one-time "Remove ads" purchase is handled entirely by Google Play, or on iPhone and iPad by the App Store. We never see your payment details. The game only learns from the store whether the purchase belongs to your store account, so it can turn the ads off, also on another device or after a new install ("Restore purchases" in Settings). Your purchase is covered by the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) or Apple's Privacy Policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). The purchase only removes ads; it never opens or skips levels.

## What the game doesn't do

- **No account with us.** Play Games and Game Center (see "Cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard") are your own Google and Apple accounts. The game itself never asks for an email address, an age, a photo or any other personal detail, and the only things you can type in are a friend's four-letter room code and, if you want one, a duel name (see "Playing a friend online").
- **No analytics about you.** Apart from the ad networks (Google, AppLovin and Meta Audience Network), the measuring of our own ads (Meta's and Tenjin's SDKs: the install and the few moments listed there), the store's purchase service and the anonymous totals, all described above, the game contains no analytics or crash-reporting software, and nothing else about how you play is sent anywhere. There is no Facebook or Instagram sign-in or sharing.
- **No permissions for your personal things.** The game doesn't use your precise location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos, and notifications only for the reminders described above, if you allow them. It uses the internet only for the ads, the measuring of our own ads, the purchase, cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard, the anonymous totals, and an online duel when you open a room.

## Children

We don't collect or keep any personal information, apart from an email you choose to send us (kept until your question or report is dealt with).

# Old Wombat Digs: Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

Old Wombat Digs is a puzzle game for Android phones and tablets and for iPhone and iPad, made by Matanya Asis, trading as As Is ("we", "us"). We are responsible for the information described here. Contact: matanya.asis288@gmail.com.

The short version: the game is free and shows ads from Google AdMob (full-screen ads between some levels and, after the first ten levels, a small banner under the game), and Google collects some device data to show and measure those ads. A one-time "Remove ads" purchase turns the ads off. Meta's SDK also counts installs from our own ads on Facebook and Instagram, and on iPhone and iPad Tenjin's SDK counts installs from our own ads shown inside other apps and games (see "Counting installs from our own ads" below). Your progress is saved only on your own device. The game adds to anonymous daily totals on our game server (like "level 12 finished today") that can't be traced to anyone. The game has no accounts, and we don't keep any personal information about you.

## What the game saves

- **Your game progress.** The levels you've finished, your best number of swipes and stars for each level, your settings (such as sound and vibration) and whether you bought "Remove ads" are saved on your device, so the game remembers where you were. This never leaves your device through the game, and it says nothing about who you are.
- **Device backup.** If device backup is on (Android backup with your Google account, or iCloud backup on iPhone and iPad), the backup may include this small save file. That backup is handled by Google or Apple under your own account, not by us, and we can't see it.
- **Deleting it.** "Reset progress" in the game's Settings clears your progress. Uninstalling the game deletes everything it saved on the device.

## Cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard

These are optional. The game plays the same without them.

- **Android (Google Play Games).** If you have a Google Play Games profile, the game can sign you in to it (automatically, or when you tap "Sign in to Play Games" in the game's Settings). It then keeps a small copy of your progress (the levels you've finished, with your best number of swipes and stars for each) in Play Games' Saved Games under your own Google Account, so you can carry on from another phone or tablet. It also tells Play Games which of the game's achievements you earned and your star count for the game's leaderboard, where other players can see it next to your Play Games gamer name. The game reads your gamer name only to show it to you in Settings. Google keeps all this as described in the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). We don't receive it and can't see it.
- **iPhone and iPad (iCloud and Game Center).** If your device is signed in to iCloud, the game keeps the same small copy of your progress in iCloud key-value storage under your Apple Account. The game never asks you to sign in for this. If your device is signed in to Game Center, the game tells Game Center which achievements you earned and your star count for the leaderboard, where other players can see it next to your Game Center nickname, as your Game Center settings allow. Apple keeps all this as described in Apple's Privacy Policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). We don't receive it and can't see it.
- **Deleting it.** On Android, delete Old Wombat Digs' data from your Play Games profile (in the Play Games app, or in your phone's Settings > Google > Play Games), or delete your whole Play Games profile in your Google Account. On iPhone and iPad, delete the game's data in Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Account Storage, and remove the game from Game Center in Settings > Game Center. "Reset progress" in the game's Settings also clears the cloud copy of your progress; scores and achievements already sent stay with Play Games or Game Center until you delete them there.

## Ads

The game shows ads from Google AdMob: full-screen ads between some levels (none on the first ten levels, and after that one every few levels you finish or fail), and, once you have finished the tenth level, a small banner ad in a strip of its own under the level list and under the burrow. No ad ever covers the burrow while you are digging.

To show ads, limit how often you see them, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's AdMob service may collect from your device:

- your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers,
- your IP address and the approximate location derived from it,
- information about the ads you see and how you interact with them,
- diagnostic and performance information about the ad service.

Google handles this information as described in "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services": https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and in the Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy. We don't receive this information and can't link it to you.

**Your choices**

- Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent before any ad is requested. You can change your choice at any time from "Privacy settings" in the game's Settings.
- On iPhone and iPad, the game asks for permission to track (Apple's App Tracking Transparency) before any personalized ads; if you choose "Ask App Not to Track", you only see non-personalized ads, and the install counts for our own ads (Meta's and Tenjin's, see below) get no advertising ID. You can change this anytime in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
- On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your device's settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads, depending on your device). Once deleted, the install count for our own ads (Meta's, see below) gets no advertising ID either.
- The one-time "Remove ads" purchase turns the ads off.

## Counting installs from our own ads

We sometimes advertise Old Wombat Digs on Facebook and Instagram, and inside other apps and games through AppLovin. To know whether those ads work, the game counts the installs they bring (Tenjin only on iPhone and iPad), with nothing else: no information about how you play or what you buy is sent, there is no sign-in, and nothing here changes which ads you see in Old Wombat Digs. The "Remove ads" purchase doesn't change this, as it shows no ads.

**Meta.** The game includes Meta's SDK (Meta Platforms, Inc.), used for one thing only: telling Meta that the game was opened, which Meta counts as an install when it's the first time on that device.

- **Who:** it is turned on only after the consent step at first launch, and on iPhone and iPad only after you have answered Apple's tracking question. Where the law requires consent first (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), it isn't turned on at all.
- **What Meta receives:** that the game was opened, the time, the game's version, basic device information (model and system version), your IP address, and your device's advertising ID: on iPhone and iPad only if you allowed tracking (if you chose "Ask App Not to Track", it is not sent, and Meta only counts installs through Apple's privacy-preserving SKAdNetwork and Meta's Aggregated Event Measurement); on Android unless you deleted the advertising ID in your device's settings.
- **What it's used for:** measuring how many people installed the game after seeing one of our ads, so we can tell which ads are worth running. Meta handles this information as described in Meta's Privacy Policy (https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy) and may use it as described there. We see only totals in Meta's ads tools, never information about you.
- **Your choices:** the tracking permission on iPhone and iPad (Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking), and on Android deleting your advertising ID in your device's settings. You can also manage how Meta uses information from other apps in your Facebook or Instagram settings ("Your activity off Meta technologies").

**Tenjin, for our ads inside other apps and games (iPhone and iPad only).** Ads shown inside other apps and games through AppLovin (AppLovin Corporation) need a measurement service in the advertised app to count installs. For this the iPhone and iPad app includes Tenjin's SDK (Tenjin, Inc.), used for one thing only: telling Tenjin that the game was opened, which Tenjin counts as an install when it's the first time on that device. No in-game events, purchases, names or other information about how you play are sent.

- **Who:** exactly the same players as Meta's install count above: only after the consent step and after you have answered Apple's tracking question, and not at all where the law requires consent first (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland).
- **What Tenjin receives:** that the game was opened, the time, the game's version, basic device information (model, iOS version, language, country and time zone settings), your IP address, and the identifier Apple gives our apps on your device (the IDFV, which only our apps can use). If you allowed tracking, also your device's advertising ID.
- **Who else:** when an install came from one of our ads, Tenjin tells the ad network that showed it (AppLovin) that the install happened, with the advertising ID only if you allowed tracking, so AppLovin can count and improve that campaign. If you chose "Ask App Not to Track", AppLovin counts installs through Apple's privacy-preserving SKAdNetwork instead, and Apple also sends Tenjin a copy of those SKAdNetwork reports, which carry no identifier and nothing about you.
- **What it's used for:** measuring how many people installed the game after seeing one of our ads, so we can tell which ads are worth running. Tenjin handles this information for us as our service provider, as described in its Privacy Policy (https://tenjin.com/privacy-policy/), and AppLovin as described in its Privacy Policy (https://www.applovin.com/privacy). We see only totals in their tools, never information about you.
- **Your choices:** the same tracking permission (Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking). To ask about or delete what Tenjin holds about your device, email us and we'll pass the request to Tenjin.

## Playing a friend online (Dig Duel)

In "Multiplayer" you can play a Dig Duel against a friend on another phone: one of you creates a room and gets a four-letter code, the other types it in. This is optional; the rest of the game, and a duel on one shared phone, work without it. Online duels use Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), a hosting service we use only to pass the duel's messages between the two phones in a room. Our Supabase project is in Frankfurt, Germany.

- **What is sent while you are in a room:** the room's four-letter code, a random ID made fresh each time the app starts (not linked to you or your device), which version of the duel your app speaks, when you came into the room, and the duel itself: which burrow is played, who swipes first, the direction of each swipe with a check number so both phones agree, and whether you ask for a rematch or leave. Your device's IP address is seen by the server to make the connection, as with any internet service.
- **No names.** There is no sign-in, no name and no chat in a duel, and the only thing you type is the room's code. The other player sees only your moves.
- **Who sees it:** the other player in the same room. Anyone who knows a room's code can join it while it is open, so give a code only to the person you want to play.
- **How long it is kept:** the messages are passed on live and are gone when the room closes. We don't store them in a database, and we don't use them for ads or profiles. Supabase may keep short-lived technical logs (such as connection records) for security, as described at https://supabase.com/privacy.

## Reminders

If you allow notifications, the game may show up to two friendly reminders when you haven't played for a while: one 2 days after you last played and one 5 days after, then nothing more until you play again (for example "Level 43 is waiting for you"). They are set by the game on your own device from your saved progress: no server sends them, and nothing about you leaves your device for them. Opening the game takes them away. Your device asks before the game can show any (after you finish level 5, never on first launch), and you can turn them off at any time with "Reminders" in the game's Settings, or in your device's settings.

## Anonymous totals

To see how the game is played and where it needs work, the game adds one to a daily total on our Supabase server (the same hosting service as for online duels, in Frankfurt, Germany) when something happens: the game opened, a phone's first play of the day (with whether it is the phone's first day with the game, the day after, or about how many days later), whether music, sounds or vibration are off, whether "Remove ads" is owned and whether the banner is showing, a level or chess level started, finished (and whether for the first time, with three stars or with opal stars), failed or left, the Chess or Multiplayer screen opened, a room made or joined, a duel started, finished or left (on one phone or online), a full-screen ad shown or one that wasn't ready, and "Remove ads" tapped, bought or restored.

All that is kept is the day, what happened, the level number where there is one, whether it was Android or iPhone/iPad, the game's version, and the running total. No ID, name, room code or device details go with it, so a total looks the same whoever added to it and can't be linked to you or to anyone else. The days your phone needs for this (its first day with the game and the last day it counted) stay on your device and are never sent. Your device's IP address is seen by the server to make the connection, as with any internet service; we don't store it with the totals or use it. We use the totals only to improve the game.

## Purchases

The optional one-time "Remove ads" purchase is handled entirely by Google Play, or on iPhone and iPad by the App Store. We never see your payment details. The game only learns from the store whether the purchase belongs to your store account, so it can turn the ads off, also on another device or after a new install ("Restore purchases" in Settings). Your purchase is covered by the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) or Apple's Privacy Policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). The purchase only removes ads; it never opens or skips levels.

## What the game doesn't do

- **No account with us.** Play Games and Game Center (see "Cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard") are your own Google and Apple accounts. The game itself never asks for a name, an email address, an age, a photo or any other personal detail, and the only thing you can type in is a friend's four-letter room code (see "Playing a friend online").
- **No analytics about you.** Apart from Google's ad service, the install counts for our own ads (Meta's and Tenjin's SDKs), the store's purchase service and the anonymous totals, all described above, the game contains no analytics or crash-reporting software, and nothing that could tell how you in particular play is sent anywhere. There is no Facebook or Instagram sign-in or sharing.
- **No permissions for your personal things.** The game doesn't use your precise location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos, and notifications only for the reminders described above, if you allow them. It uses the internet only for the ads, the install counts, the purchase, cloud save, achievements and the leaderboard, the anonymous totals, and an online duel when you open a room.

## Children
